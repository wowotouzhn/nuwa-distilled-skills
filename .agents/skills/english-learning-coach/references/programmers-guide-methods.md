## Method ideas and actionable adaptations

1. **Measure before optimizing.** Use existing diagnostic results as baseline; pick one observable outcome (e.g., correctly build five sentences, accurately retell a short passage, recognize a sound contrast). Reassess with comparable material after about a week. Avoid repeating the initial diagnostic merely because the source recommends a benchmark.
2. **Build contextual vocabulary.** Teach each useful word as a bundle: meaning in context, example, common collocation, pronunciation and a learner-produced sentence. Prefer a small number of high-value items over isolated word lists. Revisit weak items with spaced retrieval.
3. **Use input → output → correction → retry.** After a short reading or listening input, ask the learner to explain or retell it in their own English; identify the main discrepancy and let them try again. Make exercises interactive, one at a time.
4. **Practice intensive listening.** Select short, comprehensible audio *only if audio is actually available*. Ask for gist or dictation, compare with a real transcript, isolate missed words, reduced sounds, endings or grammar, then replay or reread. Never claim audio playback or listening assessment when only text is available.
5. **Train pronunciation with real feedback.** Use dictionary pronunciation models, minimal-pair awareness, word stress and shadowing as appropriate. Speech-to-text can be an imperfect *supplement*, not a definitive pronunciation score; do not pretend to have heard the learner unless audio is provided and processed.
6. **Make difficulty adaptive.** Choose understandable material with a modest challenge. If comprehension collapses, shorten or simplify. Increase complexity only after demonstrated success.
7. **Treat personal methods as hypotheses.** The source contains one author's exam journey, tool recommendations and strong opinions. They are useful starting points, not universally proven rules. Verify current tool availability, costs and test requirements separately.

## Practical 120-minute day

Preserve the existing coach schedule unless the learner requests a change:
- 20 min vocabulary in context and collocations
- 30 min grammar and independent sentence building
- 25 min intensive listening or text-based transcript work if no audio
- 25 min speaking, shadowing or spoken-response planning (do not fake pronunciation assessment)
- 15 min reading and retelling
- 5 min error review

Teach one interactive task per turn. Summarize recurring mistakes and next review steps at the end of a session.
